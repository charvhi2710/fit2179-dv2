Violent Crime in Malaysia

Where the cases sit, where the risk sits, and what those places have in common.

01 — Setting the scene

What we mean by “violent crime”

Violent crime is not one thing. It includes the everyday property losses that empty out a wallet, and the rarer, deeper violations that reach into a person directly. The Royal Malaysia Police records both, across every district in the country.

02 — Volume vs risk

Two ways to count the same country

VOLUME — raw cases

The most populated places carry the loudest count. Selangor and Kuala Lumpur — the economic centre of the country — together hold roughly a third of every violent crime recorded in 2023.

RISK — rate per 100,000

But scale is not the same thing as risk. Adjust for population and the country looks different — the states that loomed largest a moment ago shrink to ordinary, and the ones we barely noticed step forward.

03 — The flip, made visible

When the question changes, the ranking flips

If volume tells one story and risk tells another, the cleanest way to see it is side by side. On the left, every state is ranked by how many cases were recorded. On the right, by how often a case touches one in every hundred thousand residents. Two states do something dramatic.

04 — What kind of crime is where

Each state has its own crime signature

Not every state experiences the same shape of harm. Some places see crime that wears down homes and vehicles; others see crime that reaches further, into the body itself. Each state's profile tells you something about its economy, its density, and its day-to-day rhythm.

05 — Two surprises about safety

The map disagrees with the instinct — twice

The first surprise is that wealthier states are not safer overall. The second cuts the data differently: when you ask specifically where women face the highest risk of rape, the geography flips again. The poorest states are not the most dangerous, and the wealthiest places are not the safest — for women, the pattern is different still.

06 — Crime across the years

The composition has shifted

The years since 2016 tell a story of their own. Total violent crime fell sharply, and within that decline, the mix of crimes Malaysians faced has changed. The COVID-19 lockdowns left a clear footprint in the middle of the chart.

07 — Who is harmed

The line has fallen unevenly

Behind every line on this chart is a household with one fewer person at the table. The homicide rate has dropped year after year, but not at the same speed for men and women. The shaded band between the two is the difference.

08 — Where it really concentrates

Beneath the state, ten specific districts carry the volume

State-level numbers tell one story; district-level numbers tell another. The ten busiest police districts of 2023 cluster heavily in the Selangor urban belt, with single hotspots in Ipoh, Penang's Timur Laut and Kuala Lumpur's Dang Wangi. Inside every busy state, crime is concentrated in just a few places.

09 — Closing summary

A final view: the entire shape of Malaysia's 2023 violent crime as a single picture. The dominance of property crime in the country's day-to-day experience is immediately visible, as is the smaller but heavier presence of crimes against people.

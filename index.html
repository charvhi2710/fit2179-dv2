Violent Crime in Malaysia

Where the cases sit, where the risk sits, and what those places have in common.

Scope: PDRM Crime Index, 2016–2023. “Violent crime” is used in the broader Crime Index sense throughout — it covers both assault-related crime (causing injury, murder, rape, robbery) and property-related crime (break-in, vehicle theft, other theft), each labelled clearly inside every chart.

Dashboard takeaway

Volume and risk point to different places. Selangor dominates recorded cases, but rate per 100,000 pushes Kuala Lumpur and Penang forward.

52,444 recorded Crime Index cases nationwide in 2023 ~28% of assault-related cases recorded in Selangor ~80% of all cases are property-related

03 — The flip, made visible

When the question changes, the ranking flips

Volume and risk tell different stories — seen side by side, the ranking flips. On the left, states are ranked by case count; on the right, by rate per 100,000. Two states do something dramatic: KL rises to #1 by rate — the same volume falling on far fewer residents — and Penang climbs from #5 to #2 for the same reason at a smaller scale.

04 — What kind of crime is where

Each state has its own crime signature

If volume and risk disagree, what is each state actually being asked to police? Each state has a different shape of harm: its crime signature. Compare the mix within each mini-chart, not the length across states.

05 — Two surprises about safety

The map disagrees with the instinct — twice

Two patterns push back on instinct. Wealthier states are not automatically safer — density and reporting may matter more than income. For rape risk, smaller states rise to the top because fewer cases divided by smaller female populations can produce higher per-resident rates.

06 — Crime across the years

The composition has shifted

Pull back from 2023 and the picture has been changing for nearly a decade. Total recorded crime has fallen sharply since 2016, and the mix has shifted within that decline. The COVID-19 lockdowns leave a clear footprint in the middle of the chart.

07 — Who is harmed

The line has fallen unevenly

Behind every line is a household with one fewer person at the table. The homicide rate has fallen unevenly for men and women — the shaded band between is the difference.

08 — Where it really concentrates

Beneath the state, ten specific districts carry the volume

Inside every busy state, crime concentrates in just a few districts. Five of the 2023 top ten sit in Selangor's urban belt — marked here in muted purple — with further hotspots in Ipoh, Penang's Timur Laut, and KL's Dang Wangi.

How to read this chart
Inner ring Broad crime family — assault or property.
Middle ring Specific crime type within each family.
Outer ring Top state contributors for each crime type; smaller state arcs are available on hover.
Arc size Wider arc means more recorded cases in 2023.
Colour Blue shades = assault-related types; gold shades = property-related types. Outer arcs inherit the colour of their crime type.
Labels Only major arcs are labelled directly to keep the hierarchy readable.
